Overview

Turning fragmented daily observations into meaningful behavioral records

For children with developmental disabilities, everyday behavioral observations can provide important context for diagnosis and ongoing therapy. Yet these observations are often scattered across photos, videos, handwritten notes, and the memories of multiple caregivers.

KongDoc helps caregivers document behavioral patterns in a structured way, coordinate records across caregivers, and turn longitudinal observations into concise reports that can be reviewed during clinical consultations.

I led the product design and project management from early research through product definition and prototyping, working with doctors, developers, caregivers, and developmental therapists.

Background

The project began with my experience working with children with ASD.

Through volunteer programs with both typically developing children and children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), I became interested in the challenges families face while navigating developmental care.

One recurring challenge was the difficulty of accessing timely and continuous support. Therapy can be expensive, specialist wait times can be long, and caregivers often have to make day-to-day decisions with limited professional guidance.

This led our team to focus on an overlooked part of developmental care: the behavioral observations caregivers collect between clinical visits.

Problem

Fragmented behavioral records make it difficult to understand patterns over time.

Developmental conditions cannot be understood through a single medical test or clinical visit. Clinicians often rely on observations of a child’s behavior across everyday environments and over extended periods of time.

Parents commonly capture these moments through photos, videos, messages, or handwritten notes. But by the time they reach a clinical appointment, the information is often fragmented, inconsistent, and difficult to review.

The challenge was not simply helping parents record more data. It was helping them capture the right information and turn it into something useful for ongoing care.

Key Insights

Key Insights

Guardians’ parenting styles and emotional states affect their records

“When a behavioral issue happens, I’m too busy handling the situation, removing dangerous objects or calming them down…”— Caregiver C

Multiple caregivers often share roles, leading to data fragmentation.

“My child is sometimes cared for by their grandmother or a teacher, and in those cases, it’s hard to manage all the records consistently.”— Caregiver A

Uncertainty about what to report during short doctor visits

“The consultation is so short, and I never know what I should show the doctor. I just freeze up.”— Caregiver B

Data for both the doctor and the parents is needed. Track the data for long-term treatment

Experts emphasized the importance of offering the service in a digital format that enables doctors and therapists to easily review behavior logs and track progress over time.

Emotional Sensitivity of Caregivers

Caregivers often experience significant emotional stress. The product should be designed to support their emotional well-being as well as provide practical tools.

UX Writing

UX Writing principles were made to ensure emotional warmth for parents.

Warm encouragement
for emotionally burdened users

Parents of children with developmental disabilities experience the highest levels of stress among caregivers of children with illnesses or disabilities. We recognized that warm, encouraging communication is a crucial element in supporting consistent and sustainable record-keeping.

“You’re already a wonderful mom, just by being present.” “Well done! You’ve finished all your logs for the week.”

Ask questions like a kind, smart doctor

We designed our prompts to be simple and intentional—like a friendly doctor asking just the right questions—so that caregivers could feel guided, not overwhelmed.

“How did those responses from others make you feel?” “Do these symptoms or behaviors occur repeatedly?”
